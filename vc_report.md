## 1. The honest picture: how hard is this?

**Short answer: very hard as a direct graduate entry. Climate VC specifically is in a funding downturn.** Plan for VC as a 2–4 year goal, with a strong "back-door" job in September 2027.

**Why it's hard**

1. **There are very few junior seats, and most need experience.** One dedicated VC jobs board listed only **8** "Venture Capital Analyst" roles in London when indexed ([venturecapitalcareers.com](https://venturecapitalcareers.com/jobs/locations/london-eng-united-kingdom/venture-capital-analyst)). The ~80–90 "VC analyst" hits on Glassdoor are mostly generic finance roles ([Glassdoor London](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Job/london-venture-capital-analyst-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,6_IC2671300_KO7,30.htm)). Most climate VC openings I found want 1–3+ years of experience. Energy Impact Partners' London associate role asks for 1–3 years. SET Ventures' energy-tech analyst role asks for 1–2 years in VC or 2–3 in a relevant role ([SET Ventures listing](https://www.startupandvc.com/venture-capital-jobs/set-ventures-investment-analyst-4459143559)). Extantia's analyst/associate role closed 30 June 2026 ([Extantia](https://www.climatechangecareers.com/job/extantia/berlin-germany/investment-associate/)).
2. **Climate VC fundraising is weak.** A Fortune piece (28 Oct 2026) says climate VC *fundraising* is on track for its worst year since 2015 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/author/jake-rozmaryn/); it's a commentary piece, so treat the exact claim with care). PitchBook's funds data shows climate fund-raising well down from the 2021–22 peak ([PitchBook 2025 Climate Tech Funds Report](https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/2025_Climate_Tech_Funds_Report_20088.pdf)). Fewer new funds means fewer new junior hires.
3. **Deal money is concentrated.** CTVC reports climate VC investment of $26.1bn in H1 2026 (+55% YoY), but **deal count fell 25% to a five-year low** ([CTVC](https://www.ctvc.co/new-the-2026-climate-dry-powder-new-funds-report/)). Q3 2026 European tech funding was €27.8bn across 732 deals, "highly concentrated" in mega-rounds ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/10/21/europes-tech-funding-rebounds-to-eur278b-fuelled-by-billion-euro-mega-rounds/)). Bigger, fewer deals mean fewer analyst-heavy firms.
4. **Most junior VC hiring is unadvertised.** It comes through networks, portfolio companies, scouts and visiting-analyst stints. Applying cold to postings has a low success rate.

**What works in your favour**

- **Deep tech is getting a bigger share of the money.** Deep tech was ~32% of European VC in the 2026 European Deep Tech Report ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/03/24/the-2026-european-deeptech-report-sector-reaches-690b-as-vc-share-hits-record/)). Funds increasingly want technical people who can do diligence on hardware, energy and AI. **An engineer who can also model is exactly that profile.**
- **You have founder credibility.** Learn GenZ, the marine NGO and especially **Espzen AI** (self-trained vision models) give you real operator stories, which VCs value.
- **UCL is next to a live spinout fund.** The UCL Technology Fund is run by AlbionVC with UCL Business, and AlbionVC has hired past interns into analyst roles. It's also expanding spinout investing across London universities (see §5).
- **You're an EU citizen with UK residence and Singapore roots,** so you can work for both UK and EU funds without visa friction.

**How junior people actually get hired (2025–26 pattern, from the listings I reviewed)**

| Route | What it looks like | Realism for Sept 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Visiting analyst / 6-month internship → conversion | Speedinvest, Planet A, World Fund, Earlybird, Project A and Breega all run paid 6-month analyst stints | **Highest-probability direct route.** Aim for a Jan–Jun or Sept 2027 stint |
| Scout → analyst | Unpaid sourcing for a fund (e.g. First Momentum: ~1 in 15 accepted) | Good for building a record, rarely a job by itself |
| Banking/consulting/operator first, then associate after 2–3 years | Most common route into climate funds | Your backstop plan (see §7) |
| Corporate VC (CVC) internship → analyst | Siemens Energy Ventures, energy majors' venture arms | Realistic, and CVCs value engineers |
| University/spinout funds | AlbionVC/UCL Technology Fund, OSE, CIC, IP Group/Kiko, Northern Gritstone | Very realistic given UCL; deep-tech diligence suits you |

## 5. Using UCL properly

1. **UCL Technology Fund / AlbionVC.** It's the fund closest to you, it has converted interns into analysts before ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/university/ozdemir-joins-ucl-technology-fund/)), and AlbionVC is extending spinout investing across London universities ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/university/europe/uk-vc-albion-expands-spinout-investing-across-london-universities/?format=md)). Ask for a spring/summer 2027 research project or internship: for example, a technical diligence memo on a UCL engineering spinout.
2. **UCL Business (UCLB).** Offer to do market sizing or commercial assessment for physical-science spinouts as part of your MSc project or dissertation. It gives you real deal exposure and a referee.
3. **Your MSc dissertation.** Pick something an investor would read: for example, a unit-economics and financing model for long-duration storage, grid-connection queues for AI data centres, or industrial heat pumps. Publish a short version.
4. **UCL's entrepreneurship and alumni ecosystem.** Use UCL Innovation & Enterprise events and the alumni network to find UCL alumni who work at the funds in §3. A "fellow UCL engineer" note gets more replies than a cold one.
5. **Don't skip the founder angle.** Keep Espzen AI alive as a demo. VCs remember operators who shipped something.

## 7. VC-adjacent back doors (in order of how well they fit you)

1. **Energy-major commercial/strategy graduate schemes.** RWE Supply & Trading Commercial (closes 1 Feb 2027) and RWE Business Transformation & Strategy are in `jobs.csv` / `closed_or_unverified.csv`. They build energy-markets and deal skills.
2. **Corporate venture arms.** Siemens Energy Ventures (London analyst intern), bp, Shell and Equinor ventures, Future Energy Ventures. CVCs hire engineers and are a common springboard into independent climate VC.
3. **Deep-tech scale-ups in strategy/ops roles.** Riverlane, Graphcore, Helsing and Octopus Energy. Founder's-associate or BizOps roles at Series A–C climate startups are worth chasing on Climatebase and Work in Startups.
4. **Operations or strategy consulting.** Newton (£48k + £2.5k) is the most common "consulting to VC associate" path. Specialist economics shops (Frontier) are good for energy-regulation expertise.
5. **Tech transfer / spinout support.** UCLB, Oxford University Innovation, Cambridge Enterprise. This is the route into spinout funds.
6. **Climate accelerators.** Carbon13, Entrepreneur First, Antler and Climate-KIC programmes, either as an operator or, later, as a founder again.
